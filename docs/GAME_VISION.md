## 1. High Concept & One-Liner

**Living Village** is an emergent village-life simulation and artisanal agriculture sandbox where you don't just build a farm—you embed yourself in a self-sustaining, living ecosystem and generational human community that thrives, remembers, loves, struggles, and evolves across decades.

---

## 2. The Core Emotional Fantasy

Most life and farming simulations treat the town as a static backdrop: shopkeepers stand behind counters forever, dialogue repeats on predictable seasonal loops, and the player is the sole agent of change in the universe. If the player stops watering their fields, the entire world freezes.

**In Living Village, the world is alive whether you are watching or not:**
- The baker doesn't have an infinite sack of flour; they rely on grain produced by the village miller, who buys wheat from you or neighboring peasant homesteads.
- When an elder passes away, their children inherit the family orchard, dividing or expanding the land based on their skills and personal ambitions.
- When an early frost strikes, the village council convenes to discuss communal grain reserves.
- You are not the despotic ruler or the lone savior of the village; you are an essential, cherished thread woven into a rich social fabric.

> **Emotional Anchor:** *"A world that lives without you, but flourishes with you."*

## 4. Key Gameplay Pillars

### Pillar 1: Emergent Community & Social Ecology
Villagers have psychological needs, social networks, memories, and personal dreams. They form friendships, marry across families, start rivalries over land boundaries, share gossip at the local tavern, and remember favors or betrayals for years.

### Pillar 2: Deep Soil Science & Artisanal Agronomy
Farming is elevated from repetitive clicking to a rich botanical craft. Soil contains dynamic levels of Nitrogen (N), Phosphorus (P), Potassium (K), moisture, organic matter, and pH. Crop rotation, cover crops, composting, and companion planting matter deeply.

### Pillar 3: Interdependent Market & Supply Chains
Everything consumed in the village must come from somewhere. The blacksmith needs charcoal from the woodcutter and iron ore from the river hills. If crops fail, bread prices rise, forcing families to dip into grain reserves or trade treasured heirlooms.

### Pillar 4: Generational Continuity & Living History
Time advances in meaningful seasons. Children grow into apprentices, marry, build new homesteads on village outskirts, and inherit their parents' tools and reputations. The village expands naturally as prosperity increases.

## 5. Target Audience & Experience Goals

- **Target Audience:** Fans of cozy farming simulators (Stardew Valley, Rune Factory) craving deeper simulation depth; systems enthusiasts (RimWorld, Dwarf Fortress, Medieval Dynasty) looking for warm, humanistic, and non-dystopian emergent stories.
- **Session Feel:**
  - *Short Term (15–30 mins):* Tactile satisfaction of harvesting, planting, chatting with neighbors, tending livestock, and fulfilling market orders.
  - *Medium Term (2–5 hours):* Navigating seasonal shifts, upgrading production facilities, courtships, community festivals, and trading caravans.
  - *Long Term (20–100+ hours):* Watching a humble hamlet grow into a thriving township across generations, leaving an enduring family legacy.
